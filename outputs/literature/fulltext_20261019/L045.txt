Source Localization Based on FDOA and TDOA
With Bounded Sensor Location Errors and
Changing Carrier Frequency
Jonghoek Kim
Abstract—This study tackles locating a noncooperative source
based on passive measurements of multiple sonar sensors, such
that every sonar sensor can measure both frequency difference
of arrival (FDOA) and time difference of arrival (TDOA) of the
source’s sound signal. This article considers a scenario where
the unknown carrier frequency of the source may vary as time
elapses. Furthermore, there can be large errors in underwater
sensor localization, since global positioning system (GPS) cannot
be used in underwater environments. This study addresses
FDOA-TDOA localization under the assumption that a sensor’s
location error is bounded above and that the speed limit of the
source is accessible a priori. As far as we know, this manuscript is
novel in solving FDOA-TDOA localization, considering scenarios
where a sensor’s location error exists and unknown carrier
frequency can vary as time elapses. In addition, our research is
novel in not requiring a prior information on the FDOA-TDOA
measurement noise power. The effectiveness of the proposed
hybrid FDOA-TDOA localization scheme is demonstrated by
comparing it with the state-of-the-art hybrid FDOA-TDOA
localization through extensive computer-based simulations.
Index Terms—Changing carrier frequency, FDOA-time differ-
ence of arrival (TDOA), frequency difference of arrival (FDOA),
noise power information, sensor location error, underwater
source localization, unknown carrier frequency.

can be easily detected by a noncooperative target, such as an
enemy submarine. Therefore, passive sonar measurements are
important in underwater tracking of a noncooperative target.
This article deals with locating a noncooperative under-
water source utilizing multiple passive sonar sensors. It is
assumed that every sonar sensor can measure both frequency
difference of arrival (FDOA) and time difference of arrival

References [9], [10], and [11] addressed how to ﬁx the
source estimation, in the presence of sensor position errors.
However, [9], [10], and [11] assumed that sensor position
error has a Normal distribution with known standard deviation
(STD).
In practice, distribution of sensor position error may not be
accessible. In this article, we assume that the bound of sensor
position error is accessible. For instance, using three-sigma
rules, almost 99% of all observed data fall within three STDs
of the mean. Hence, three STDs can be set as the bound for
sensor position error. Thus, we argue that a known bound for
sensor position error is more general than a known distribution
for sensor position error.
Our study solves hybrid FDOA-TDOA localization, con-
sidering scenarios where a sensor’s location error is bounded
above and unknown carrier frequency can vary as time
elapses. In this article, a pseudo-location estimate list (PLL)
is generated by adding a bounded random pseudo noise to a
sensor’s location estimate. Here, a bounded random pseudo
noise is generated considering the known bounds on sensor
location errors.
Considering the fact that a source vehicle cannot move
with inﬁnite speed, this article addresses hybrid FDOA-TDOA
localization under the assumption that the speed limit of the
source is accessible a priori. Considering the speed limit
of the source, this article computes the viable frequency
interval in which the carrier frequency can exist. Within the
viable frequency interval, one computes a set of viable carrier
frequencies. Every viable carrier frequency and PLL lead to
an FDOA-TDOA least squares solution. Among all source
solutions, one searches for a source solution which yields the
minimum residual error. In this manner, one computes the most
viable source solution.
As far as we know, our study is novel in solving hybrid
FDOA-TDOA localization, considering scenarios where a
sensor’s location error exists and unknown carrier frequency
can vary as time elapses. Moreover, this article is novel
in not requiring a prior information on the FDOA-TDOA
measurement noise power. Through computer-based simula-
tions, the effectiveness of the proposed localization scheme is
demonstrated by comparison with the state-of-the-art hybrid
FDOA-TDOA localization algorithm in [12].

II. LITERATURE REVIEW
This study solves hybrid FDOA-TDOA localization, con-
sidering scenarios where a sensor’s location error exists and
unknown carrier frequency can vary as time elapses. Recall
that the carrier frequency deﬁnes the original frequency of the
emitted signal.
There are many papers on the source localization through
hybrid FDOA-TDOA measurements [3], [13], [14], [15],
[16], [17], [18], [19]. Reference [3] addressed an algebraic
solution for the source localization through FDOA-TDOA
measurements. Reference [15] addressed the source localiza-
tion through FDOA-TDOA measurements, by formulating a
weighted least squares (WLSs) problem. Zhang et al. [17]
addressed the hybrid FDOA-TDOA source localization, which
handles unknown signal propagation speed and sensor location
errors. References [12] and [14] addressed the hybrid FDOA-
TDOA localization method, in the presence of sensor location
uncertainties. Through converting the measurements with
respect to inaccurate sensor locations, [12] and [14] intro-
duced a WLS scheme for computing the source solution. For
solving hybrid FDOA-TDOA source localization under sensor
location errors, [20] used semideﬁnite programming (SDP),
which converts the WLS problem to a convex optimization
problem.
All the mentioned-above approaches rely on the exact
knowledge of the FDOA-TDOA measurement noise power,
which is usually not available in practice. Even if there is only
a slight error in the noise power, these localization approaches
may suffer severe performance degradation. In this article,
we do not require a prior information on the FDOA-TDOA
measurement noise power. As far as we know, this article is
novel in not requiring a prior information on the FDOA-TDOA
measurement noise power.
All the mentioned-above approaches considered the case
in which the carrier frequency of a noncooperative source is
accessible a priori. Note that this known carrier frequency
assumption is not used in this article.
A passive sonar sensor measures the variation of source
frequency, but the carrier frequency of the source is not
measured by a sonar sensor. A noncooperative source’s carrier
frequency may not be accessible in advance, and the carrier
frequency may vary as time elapses. For example, consider
the case where the carrier frequency is generated by the
source’s propeller, such as a submarine’s propeller. As the
propeller’s rotation speed changes as time goes on, the carrier
frequency can change [5], [6], [8]. Witzgall [4] showed that
the source solution is strongly dependent on the source’s
unknown original carrier frequency.
As far as we know, this article is novel in solving hybrid
FDOA-TDOA localization, considering the case where a sen-
sor’s location error is bounded above and that unknown carrier
frequency varies as time elapses. Furthermore, this article is
novel in not requiring a prior information on the FDOA-
TDOA measurement noise power. The outperformance of the
proposed hybrid localization scheme is veriﬁed by comparison
with [12] through computer-based simulations.

VI. CONCLUSION
This research tackles localization of a source under FDOA-
TDOA measurements, in the case where there are large errors
in sensor location estimates. Furthermore, we consider the case
in which the unknown carrier frequency varies as time elapses.
As far as we know, this study is novel in proposing FDOA-
TDOA localization under the assumption that a sensor’s
location error is bounded above and that the unknown carrier
frequency varies as time elapses. In addition, this article is
novel in not requiring a prior information on the FDOA-TDOA
measurement noise power.
Using computer-based simulations, this study demonstrates
the outperformance of the proposed location scheme by com-
paring it with the state-of-the-art FDOA-TDOA algorithm. In
the future, we will run experiments with real sonar sensors, for
demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed localization
scheme.
