IV. CONCLUSION
We have introduced a generalization of the matched subspace ﬁlters
for the detection of unknown signals in a background of non-Gaussian,
nonindependent noise. To do so the already known detector based on
the Rao test, which is applicable to non-Gaussian and independent
noise, is modiﬁed to include a previous linear transformation based
on ICA. In order to obtain general applicability of the proposed test,
a nonparametric estimation of the noise PDF is used after the linear
transformation. Thereby, the properties of the Rao test are preserved
for any kind of non-Gaussian noise distribution. Several experiments
were conducted with simulated examples in order to evaluate the MSF
in comparison to the GMSF. The study of the resulting ROC curves
show the performance enhancement obtained with the generalized so-
lution, and, more speciﬁcally that the degradation of the MSF in pres-
ence of dependent noise cannot be easily compensated except by using
the GMSF.

An Asymptotically Efﬁcient Estimator for TDOA and
FDOA Positioning of Multiple Disjoint Sources in the
Presence of Sensor Location Uncertainties
Ming Sun and K. C. Ho
Abstract—This correspondence considers the problem of locating
multiple disjoint sources using time differences of arrival (TDOAs) and
frequency differences of arrival (FDOAs) in the presence of sensor posi-
tion and velocity errors. Previous work applies to one source only with
suboptimum performance for near source, or requires joint estimation of
the source and sensor locations that could be computation demanding.
Through nonlinearly transforming the measurements and converting
them with respect to the inaccurate sensor locations, we have developed
an algebraic solution to this problem. The solution is shown analytically
to achieve the Cramér–Rao lower bound (CRLB) performance over small
noise region and does not require joint estimation with sensor locations.
Index Terms—Disjoint sources, frequency difference of arrival (FDOA),
sensor location uncertainty, source localization, time difference of arrival
(TDOA).
I. INTRODUCTION
For several decades, the problem of source localization using time
differences of arrival (TDOAs) has received considerable interest
in many ﬁelds, such as radar, sonar, navigation, and sensor net-
works [1]–[4]. Modern localization system often uses dynamic mobile
platforms as receivers. Mobile platform has the advantage of creating
frequency differences of arrival (FDOA) from the Doppler effect due
to the relative motion between the source and receivers, which can be
exploited to improve the localization accuracy. An undesirable effect
is that the mobile receivers will have errors in their positions and
velocities.
Localization of a stationary or moving source using TDOA and
FDOA is not a trivial task due to the nonlinear nature of the estima-
tion problem [5]–[8]. Ho and Xu [7] utilized the advantage of having
TDOA available in forming the FDOA solution equation and as a result
came up with a closed-form solution to solve the localization problem
that achieves the Cramér–Rao lower bound (CRLB) performance
at small noise region. Reference [9] proposed another closed-form
estimator by exploiting the multidimentional scaling analysis. In the
presence of sensor location errors, [8] extended the method in [7] to
account for the uncertainty in sensor locations. The resulted estimator

is able to reach the CRLB accuracy for a distant but not a near source.
Sun et al. [10] applied the total least-squares method to this problem.
However, their solution cannot attain the CRLB accuracy for either
distant or near source. All of these works consider one source only.
We address in this correspondence the problem of the localization of
multiple disjoint sources, where the disjointness could come from time,
frequency or both so that their positioning parameters (e.g., TDOA) can
be obtained separately, but noise correlation could exist among them.
This is a simpler problem than non-disjoint source localization where
the sources could interact with one another and no separate positioning
parameters can be obtained. An interesting observation about the mul-
tiple disjoint sources is that they exhibit the same sensor location error
and it is possible to reduce the performance loss caused by sensor lo-
cation error. The recent work [11] explored this notion and developed
an approximately efﬁcient estimator to locate multiple disjoint sources
using TDOA with sensor position uncertainty. The method has high
computation complexity because it requires hypothesized source loca-
tions and estimates jointly the sensor and source positions. In the situa-
tion where only the source locations are of interest or limited computa-
tion capacity is available, applying this estimator could be prohibitive.
Other alternatives for multiple disjoint source estimation such as itera-
tive maximum likelihood estimator [12] may suffer from the problems
of local convergence and divergence.
This correspondence proposes an estimator for the localization of
multiple disjoint moving sources using TDOAs and FDOAs when the
sensor locations, in both positions and velocities, have errors. The ap-
proach taken here is not to obtain the source locations individually
which is possible because we have separate measurements for different
sources, but rather to estimate all the source locations together simulta-
neously. The purpose is to improve performance by suppressing better
the sensor locations errors. In addition, estimating them together is
able to account for the possible noise correlation in the positioning
parameters of different sources to achieve better results. We ﬁrst es-
tablish the pseudolinear equations by introducing nuisance parameters
and solve them by using a two-stage processing approach. The pro-
posed estimator has closed-form and does not require joint estimation
with sensor locations. Furthermore, it is proven analytically to reach the
CRLB performance for both distant and near sources when the noise
level is small. This correspondence is an extension of the early work
[8] by considering the localization of multiple disjoint sources rather
than a single source. The idea is similar to [8] by using weighting ma-
trix to account for the sensor position error. However, the solution de-
velopment is better and different, leading to the result that even for the
single source scenario our estimator reaches the CRLB performance no
matter where the source is while the one in [8] can only attain the CRLB
accuracy for distant source only. The developed estimator here is much
more computationally attractive than the one in [11] because joint es-
timation with sensor positions and the use of hypothesized source po-
sitions are not needed. Furthermore, [11] only examines TDOA local-
ization of stationary sources with ﬁxed sensors.
The rest of the correspondence is organized as follows. Section II
formulates the localization problem mathematically. In Section III,
the proposed solution is developed. Section IV conducts the perfor-
mance analysis. The special case of locating stationary sources using
TDOA is elaborated in Section V. The theoretical developments are
supported by simulation in Section VI. Section VII concludes the
correspondence.

Both the proposed algorithm and the method in [11] utilize the two-
stage approach to obtain closed-form solution where the complexity is
dominated by the ﬁrst stage. The main difference between them lies in
the ﬁrst stage where the proposed estimator ﬁnds the source position
only while [11] jointly estimates the source and sensor positions. Their
ﬁrst stages essentially compute the WLS solution which requires the
inverse of a matrix having a size equal to 4K in the proposed algo-
rithm (3K for the K source coordinates in 3-D and 1 auxiliary vari-
able for each source) and 4K + 3M in the case of [11] where 3M
comes from the estimation of M sensor positions. Therefore, the com-
putation complexity is O((4K)3) for the proposed solution and it is
O((4K + 3M)3) for the method in [11], where M needs to be larger
than 4 in 3-D positioning. The difference in complexity could be very
signiﬁcant when M=K is large.
While the proposed algorithm can be easily adapted to the case for
TDOA only localization, the modiﬁcation for the FDOA only case is
not straightforward and reformulating the entire approach is needed.

VII. CONCLUSION
In this correspondence, a closed-form solution is proposed to locate
multiple disjoint sources in the presence of random sensor location er-
rors. A set of pseudolinear equations from TDOA and FDOA measure-
ments is established with respect to the available but noisy sensor lo-
cations and they are solved by the two-stage approach. Although no
