Sensor position and velocity uncertainties are known to be able to degrade the source localization 
accuracy signiﬁcantly. This paper focuses on the problem of locating multiple disjoint sources using 
time differences of arrival (TDOAs) and frequency differences of arrival (FDOAs) in the presence of 
sensor position and velocity errors. First, the explicit Cramér–Rao bound (CRB) expression for joint 
estimation of source and sensor positions and velocities is derived under the Gaussian noise assumption. 
Subsequently, we compare the localization accuracy when multiple-source positions and velocities are 
determined jointly and individually based on the obtained CRB results. The performance gain resulted 
from multiple-target cooperative positioning is also quantiﬁed using the orthogonal projection matrix. 
Next, the paper proposes a new estimator that formulates the localization problem as a quadratic 
programming with some indeﬁnite quadratic equality constraints. Due to the non-convex nature of the 
optimization problem, an iterative constrained weighted least squares (ICWLS) method is developed 
based on matrix Q R decomposition, which can be achieved through some simple and eﬃcient numerical 
algorithms. The newly proposed iterative method uses a set of linear equality constraints instead 
of the quadratic constraints to produce a closed-form solution in each iteration. Theoretical analysis 
demonstrates that the proposed method, if converges, can provide the optimal solution of the formulated 
non-convex minimization problem. Moreover, its estimation mean-square-error (MSE) is able to reach the 
corresponding CRB under moderate noise level. Simulations are included to corroborate and support the 
theoretical development in this paper.

1. Introduction
The problem of emitter localization has attracted considerable interest during the past several decades. Indeed, it has found numerous 
applications in many areas including signal processing, wireless communications, sonar, surveillance, navigation, geophysics, passive radar, 
microphone array processing, and so on [1–6]. Most localization systems require two estimation steps. In the ﬁrst step, some interme-
diate parameters that are embedded in the received signals are extracted at several stations or different time slots through some signal 
processing techniques. In the second step, the source location is determined by ﬁnding the coordinate that best ﬁts the lines of position 
(LOP) associated with the parameters obtained in the ﬁrst step.
For a stationary emitter, source localization can be based on angle of arrival (AOA) [7–9], time difference of arrival (TDOA) [10–12], 
time of arrival (TOA) [13–15], received signal strength (RSS) [16–18] measurements, or a combination of them [19]. If there exists relative 
motion between the sensors and the source, frequency difference of arrival (FDOA) [20–35] metric can also be incorporated to improve 
the location accuracy. One of the most accurate and common methods for emitter localization is based on TDOA and FDOA. The reason is 
that combination of TDOA and FDOA measurements can facilitate localization of the source in a wide range of bandwidth. Moreover, they

can be jointly estimated from the cross-correlation of signals received by several pairs of sensors [36–38]. In this paper, we focus on the 
TDOA/FDOA-based source localization problem.
In recent years, a number of localization methods based on TDOA/FDOA measurements become available in the literature. Perhaps the 
most representative approach is the two-step weighted least squares (TSWLS) algorithm [20], which is able to ﬁnd a closed-form solution 
for determining the position and velocity of a moving source. Besides, an improved TSWLS algorithm is proposed in [21] to reduce the 
localization errors at high noise level. Multidimensional scaling (MDS) analysis [22] is an alternative technique to provide the algebraic 
solution for moving source location using TDOA and FDOA metrics. Compared to the classical TSWLS algorithm, the threshold effect of the 
MDS analysis algorithm occurs apparently later as the measurement noise increases for a near-ﬁeld target [22]. Certainly, methods with 
closed-form solution are attractive because they do not have divergence problem and have low computational complexities. However, the 
algebraic solutions can reach the Cramér–Rao bound (CRB) accuracy only at relatively low noise level. In order to improve the localization 
accuracy under suﬃciently small signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) condition, the iterative techniques should be employed. Speciﬁcally, the semi-
deﬁnite relaxation (SDR) method is proposed in [23], which relaxes the non-convex TDOA/FDOA-based localization problem. Although 
this method does not suffer from local convergence, it generally requires a high computational complexity for reasonable performance. 
Additionally, an eﬃcient constrained total least squares (CTLS) method for TDOA and FDOA localization is proposed in [24]; a constrained 
weighted least squares (CWLS) method for moving source location using TDOA/FDOA measurements is developed in [25]. Both of them 
require Newton iteration, which does not guarantee convergence to the global optimum. Quite recently, an iterative constrained weighted 
least squares (ICWLS) method is proposed in [26], in which a linearization procedure on the quadratic equality constraints is iteratively 
accomplished to yield an approximate programming with linear constraints. Moreover, the weighting matrix is updated in each iteration. 
Both theoretical analysis and simulation studies demonstrate that, compared with the original CWLS method, the ICWLS method has 
better convergence behavior and higher noise threshold.
It is worth pointing out that all of the above-mentioned works assume the sensor positions and velocities are known accurately. 
However, in practical applications the sensor positions and/or velocities available for the positioning task may have errors. For instance, 
the sensors can be airplanes or unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) whose positions and velocities may not be precisely known. In addition, 
inaccurate sensor position occurs when the receivers are randomly deployed in a ﬁeld whose positions need to be measured or when 
the sensors drift over a period of time. Unfortunately, regardless of the localization algorithms used, the source location accuracy can be 
very sensitive to the accurate knowledge of the receiver positions and velocities. As a result, much attention has been paid to the emitter 
location problem in the presence of sensor position and velocity errors. In [27,28], a theoretical study is performed when an optimum 
estimator using TDOA/FDOA measurements assumes the sensor positions and velocities are exact but, in fact, they have errors. Generally, 
there exist two classes of TDOA/FDOA-based localization methods that can mitigate the effects of the uncertainties in sensor position 
and velocity. The ﬁrst one takes the statistical characteristic of sensor position and velocity uncertainties into the localization procedure 
to improve estimation accuracy. Speciﬁcally, in [29], a total least squares (TLS) method for TDOA and FDOA positioning is proposed in 
the presence of sensor position and velocity errors; in [30], a CWLS method for the same localization scenario is developed. The second 
one performs joint estimation of the unknown source position and velocity as well as the inaccurate sensor positions and velocities. For 
example, in [31,32], the Taylor-series technique for moving source localization using TDOA/FDOA measurements is presented with sensor 
position and velocity uncertainties. It should be noted that the methods in the second class can increase the accuracy of sensor position 
and velocity estimates and tolerate higher noise level before the thresholding effect starts to occur.
On the other hand, all the localization methods previously discussed only consider the single source case. Recent works [11,16,33–35]
have developed some algebraic solutions for locating multiple disjoint sources when the sensor positions and velocities are not known 
accurately. The disjointness could come from time, frequency or both so that their positioning parameters (e.g., TDOA and FDOA) can be 
obtained separately. Nevertheless, noise correlation could still exist among those localization parameters. An important observation about 
the multiple disjoint sources is that they exhibit the same sensor position and velocity errors. As a result, the multiple sources should be 
jointly located based on the measurements that are subject to the same sensor position displacements. This procedure can be viewed as 
multiple-target cooperative positioning. This paper addresses the problem of locating multiple disjoint moving sources using TDOAs and 
FDOAs in the presence of erroneous sensor positions and velocities. Indeed, there have been several methods developed for this localization 
scenario. In [33], an algebraic solution to this problem is proposed and the solution is shown analytically to achieve the Cramér–Rao bound 
(CRB) performance over small noise region. However, this method cannot provide optimum accuracy for sensor positions and velocities. 
Following the work of [33], an alternative closed-form solution is presented in [34], which improves the previous method so that both the 
source and the receiver position and velocity estimates can reach the CRB accuracy. In addition, an algebraic solution for locating multiple 
disjoint sources with the use of calibration emitters is proposed in [35]. It should be noted that all the proposed estimators in [33–35] are 
in closed-form and they do not have a large amount of noise tolerance before the performance deviates suddenly from the CRB due to the 
nonlinear nature of the source localization problem. In order to improve the noise threshold, we need to resort to the iterative technique.
In this work, aiming at the multiple-target cooperative localization with the use of TDOA/FDOA measurements, we propose an asymp-
totically eﬃcient estimator that formulates the problem as a quadratic programming with some indeﬁnite quadratic equality constraints. 
Similar to [26], we also develop an ICWLS method to obtain the approximate solution of the quadratic programming. But, the presented 
procedure can locate multiple disjoint sources in the presence of sensor position and velocity errors. Moreover, the new method is based 
on matrix Q R decomposition, which is implemented through some simple and eﬃcient numerical algorithms. The iterative method uses 
a set of linear equality constraints instead of the quadratic constraints to yield an explicit solution in each iteration. The theoretical per-
formance of the proposed ICWLS method is also derived. The theoretical study consists of two parts: convergence property and estimation 
mean-square-error (MSE). The results indicate that the proposed method, if converges, can provide the optimal solution of the formulated 
minimization problem. Moreover, its estimation MSE is able to achieve the corresponding CRB under moderate noise level. Some numerical 
experiments are conducted to conﬁrm the effectiveness of the theoretical analysis and demonstrate advantage of the proposed method.
The main novelty and contribution of this paper are summarized below:
(1) We prove analytically through the CRB analysis that the cooperative gain is considerable when the multiple disjoint moving sources 
are located simultaneously based on TDOA/FDOA metrics. This result means that the positions and velocities of the multiple moving 
sources should be jointly estimated in practical application even if the sources are disjoint.

5. Proposed ICWLS estimator
In this section, a new ICWLS method is presented. We ﬁrst derive the TDOA/FDOA pseudo-linear equations as well as the quadratic 
equation constraints. Subsequently, a new CWLS estimator is formulated. In order to obtain the optimal solution to the estimator, an 
eﬃcient iterative algorithm based on Q R decomposition is developed, which can provide a closed-form solution and update the weighting 
matrix in each iteration.

Remark 10. If the optimization problem is non-convex, it is very important to ﬁnd a proper starting point for arbitrary iterative algorithm. 
The reason for this is that the initial guess would affect the convergence speed and estimation accuracy. For the proposed algorithm, we 
need to choose an appropriate initial estimate for ˜t through the least squares (LS) minimization. It can be determined by ignoring the 
equality constraints and taking the weighting matrix W as identity matrix. As a consequence, we have

Although this solution is not asymptotically optimal, it is suﬃciently precise as an initial estimate for global convergence. It is worth 
noting that there is no systematic method of yielding a good starting point so that the algorithm always converges to the global optimum. 
However, the simulation results in Section 7 demonstrate that using this initial estimate can lead to satisfactory performance.
Remark 11. It can be observed from Fig. 2 that the updated value in the k + 1th iteration is the linear combination of the solution to 
problem (76) and the estimation result in the kth iteration. A large number of simulation results demonstrate that as long as w1 is not 
close to 1, the ﬁnal estimation accuracy is not sensitive to the numerical value of w1. In our simulations, we set w1 = w2 = 0.5.
Remark 12. Although the weighting matrix W is updated per iteration, its true value cannot be obtained anyway. The reason is that 
calculating W requires ¯r and ¯s, but they are not available in practice. So we have to update W using ˆ¯r and ˆ¯s, which are subject to noises. 
Fortunately, the approximation of the weighting matrix has an insigniﬁcant effect on the ﬁnal estimation accuracy. This observation agrees 
with the ﬁndings in [41–43]. Our simulation results in Section 7 also verify this conclusion.

(1) The proposed ICWLS solution can achieve the corresponding CRB under a moderate noise level. The reason is that the new estimator 
is formed based on the WLS criterion, which is able to provide an asymptotically eﬃcient solution under Gaussian noise assumption. In 
Subsection 6.2 we prove analytically that the presented method can reach the CRB accuracy for Gaussian data model at moderate noise 
level. Therefore, the simulation results support and corroborate the theoretical development in Subsection 6.2.
(2) The presented solution has a higher noise threshold than the other methods, because of two reasons. First, the weighting matrix 
can be updated per iteration in the proposed estimator. As a result, when the noise level becomes large the asymptotic performance can 
be improved. Second, the proposed method has the advantage that there is no need to select step-size because the optimal solution in 
closed-form is available for each iteration. Indeed, when the step-size is badly chosen, it will lead to divergence. Fortunately, this problem 
does not exist in the new method.
(3) The Taylor-series method also yields asymptotically eﬃcient solutions if it is initialized with the true value. However, when the 
initial guesses of the Taylor-series method are randomly generated, it departs from the CRB earlier than the other methods, including the 
TSWLS method. It is in fact diﬃcult to make an initial guess close to the true value for the Taylor-series method because the pseudo-linear 
vector equation is not available for this method and the number of unknowns is large in the presence of sensor position and velocity 
uncertainties.
(4) The estimation performance of the CWLS method in [30] is nearly the same as that of the CTLS method in [45]. This ﬁnding is 
reasonable since the latter is the extended version of the former in multiple-source localization scenario.
(5) The TSWLS algorithm breaks away from the CRB earlier than the CWLS method, which is consistent with the observation in [30]. 
The likely reason for this is that the TSWLS algorithm may generate complex values when ﬁnding the square root in its second stage. 
However, the TSWLS algorithm is more computationally eﬃcient and does not require initialization and iteration.

8. Conclusion
This paper concentrates on the problem of locating multiple disjoint sources using TDOAs and FDOAs in the presence of sensor position 
and velocity uncertainties. The CRB expression for joint estimation of source and sensor positions and velocities is derived under the Gaus-
sian noise assumption. The performance improvement due to multiple-target cooperative positioning is quantiﬁed based on the obtained 
CRB. Subsequently, an asymptotically eﬃcient estimator that formulates the problem as a quadratic programming with some indeﬁnite 
quadratic equality constraints is presented. Similar to [26], we also develop an ICWLS method to obtain the approximate solution of the 
quadratic programming. But, the proposed procedure can locate multiple disjoint sources in the presence of sensor position and velocity 
errors. Moreover, the new method is based on matrix Q R decomposition, which can be achieved through some simple and eﬃcient 
numerical algorithms. Besides, the newly proposed iterative algorithm uses a set of linear equality constraints instead of the quadratic 
constraints to produce a closed-form solution for each iteration. The theoretical performance of the proposed ICWLS estimator is also de-
rived. The theoretical study contains two parts: convergence property and estimation MSE. The results indicate that the proposed method,
