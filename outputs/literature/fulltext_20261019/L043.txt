Joint Sensor Array Path Planning and Attitude
Determination for Optimal Emitter Localization
Pan Li , Jianfeng Li , Member, IEEE, Fuhui Zhou , Senior Member, IEEE,
Xiaofei Zhang , and Qihui Wu, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract— Existing path planning schemes designed for wire-
less sensor networks generally account for abstract payload
sensors, rendering them inapplicable to concrete sensor-array-
based localization systems due to differences in measurement
models. In this paper, we establish a general framework for path
planning of a practical sensor array and factor in an oft-neglected
degree of freedom regarding optimality, i.e., the array’s orien-
tation/attitude. The optimization problem is formulated based
on the A-optimality criterion under constraints arising from
the maximum distance between consecutive waypoints, maximal
heading change, and forbidden regions. To facilitate semidefinite
relaxation (SDR), we recast the optimization function into a
fractional nonhomogeneous quadratic structure and transform
the constraints into a bilinear form. By applying SDR and
replacing the bilinear terms with a matrix variable, the problem
is relaxed into a single-ratio fractional program. By leveraging
the Charnes-Cooper variable transformation, we transform the
single-ratio fractional program into a mixed semidefinite/second-
order cone program (SD/SOCP) that can be solved in polynomial
time. Finally, we apply the results to angle-of-arrival (AOA) and
direct localization. Simulation results demonstrate that the pro-
posed path planning scheme attains near-optimal performance.

location by triangulation of bearing lines, a.k.a. angle-of-

in the past few decades [6]. Employing an antenna array and
performing AOA estimation by harnessing the second/fourth-

techniques may discard information in the AOA measurement

direct position determination (DPD), was introduced in the
seminal works by Weiss [8] as a remedy for this problem. The
direct localization algorithm estimates the emitter position in
a fusion center by jointly processing all the array outputs from
each array. Consequently, the direct localization algorithm can
prevent potential information loss to some extent since the
intermediate parameter estimation procedure is circumvented.
Both empirical and theoretical studies have demonstrated
the practicality and efficacy of semidefinite relaxation (SDR)
for addressing NP-hard quadratic problems [9].
The SDR
has wide-ranging applications in various engineering and sci-
ence fields, such as digital communication (e.g., the Boolean
least-squares problem in code division multiple access [10]
and the downlink transmit beamforming problems [11]), radar
(e.g., adaptive radar detection [12] and coded waveform design
[13]), and speech recognition (e.g., the large-margin parameter
estimation problem [14]). Mathematical justifications for this

numerical results from prior studies confirm its efficacy in
addressing problems associated with bilinear structures.

observability and increase localization accuracy. Consequently,
path planning schemes for mobile payload sensors have
been extensively studied in a vast amount of literature
[16], [17], [18], [19], [20], [21], [22], [23]. Both the covariance
matrix and the Fisher information matrix (FIM) can be used
as the optimization criterion to assess the uncertainty of the
localization result. Although conceptually simple, the former
method can be complex and challenging to execute, primarily
due to the dense structure of the covariance matrix in most
circumstances. Consequently, using scalar measures of the
FIM as the criterion to evaluate uncertainty is the top-priority
choice in path planning problems. Three leading criteria, i.e.,
A-optimality (minimize the trace of the inverse of the FIM),
D-optimality (maximize the determinant of the FIM), and

In [16], the authors proposed an unconstrained path planning
approach for a single AOA sensor. This paper employs the
D-optimality criterion as its optimality index while assum-
ing that the noisy AOA measurements are Gaussian random
variables with constant variances. This assumption assumes
that measurement errors remain consistent both near and far
from the emitter. For the optimal localization of a stationary
underwater target, an unconstrained path design strategy for a
single movable sensor that collects range measurements was
proposed in [17]. The path planning approach for scan-based
measurements was proposed by Doˇgancay in [18]. Oshman
and Davidson proposed a constrained path planning scheme
for an AOA sensor subject to state constraints imposed on
the trajectory [19]. The optimization criterion and AOA mea-
surement model employed in this paper are identical to those
used in [16], and numerical techniques such as differential
inclusion are used to solve the path design problem. In [20],
Doˇgancay proposed a local optimal path planning scheme
for heterogeneous payload sensors (encompassing AOA, time-
difference-of-arrival (TDOA), and scan-based sensors) under
physical and geometric path constraints based on the D-
optimality criterion. The author adopted a more practical
measurement model in this paper, which assumes that the
noisy AOA measurements follow a Gaussian distribution with
variances depending on the distance. In [21], He et al. pro-
posed an unconstrained robust path planning scheme for a
single AOA sensor using the greedy gradient descent method
to mitigate the impact of sensor location errors. The mea-
surement model and optimization criterion are similar to [20].
By harnessing the bilinear structure of the FIM, SDR has
recently been extended in [22] to time-of-arrival (TOA) sensor
path planning problems. In this paper, Tzoreff and Weiss
considered constraints like speed limits and no-fly zones while
using the E-optimality criterion as the performance index.
The two authors addressed the path design problem for two
TDOA mobile sensors by utilizing numerical algorithms such
as the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM),
majorization-minimization (MM) algorithm, and SDR while
imposing constraints, such as boundaries on the minimum and
maximum sensor distances, no-fly zones, and maneuvering
limitations [23].
In summary, this brief review of the state-of-the-art path
planning schemes indicates that existing methods exploit a
general formulation whereby abstract sensors whose mea-
surements are corrupted by Gaussian noise are used for
localization. This frequently used framework lacks applica-
bility in a practical sensor array localization system due to
its somewhat abstract and misleading assumptions regarding
the measurement model. Besides, most of the research efforts
invested in path planning problems only deal with the choice of
sensor positions. In fact, movable sensors can offer additional
degrees of freedom regarding optimality, for example, rotation
[24]. Besides, geometry-constrained path planning schemes
for AOA sensors may get stuck in a local optimum since
there are no existing global solvers to the best of the author’s
knowledge. To alleviate these issues, we propose a path
planning scheme for an antenna array by leveraging the idea of
SDR, which is applicable to both AOA and direct localization.
The key contributions in this paper are as follows.
• Prior research on path planning has often formulated
the problem using overly simplified models of AOA
measurements, such as those based on constant or
distance-dependent variance. While such models have
the benefit of being straightforward to optimize, they
might not be sufficiently accurate to reflect practical
situations.
To address this problem, we propose a rig-
orous formulation of the path planning problem for an
antenna array with an arbitrary structure under several
geometric constraints based on a practical FIM derived
from the raw array outputs. Additionally, unlike current
path planning schemes that focus primarily on selecting
waypoint positions, our approach involves considering the
orientation of the sensor array (i.e., how it rotates) at
every waypoint to achieve improved performance.
• The existing constrained AOA path planning scheme has
no computational complexity guarantees. To address this
problem, the SDR technique is employed for obtaining
a near-optimal solution to the proposed optimization
problem, which does not require an initial point in
close proximity to the optimum to avoid local con-
vergence or divergence. Besides, the SDR technique
constitutes a mixed semidefinite/second-order cone pro-
gram (SD/SOCP), which can be solved in polynomial
time. Consequently, the proposed path planning scheme
has guaranteed computational complexity performance.
• Because the path planning scheme requires values of the
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and the received signal power
model parameters, we propose a functioning method
to estimate these parameters. Past literature considered
abstract sensors, and these parameter uncertainties are
usually ignored and assumed as a priori. Specifically,
we apply the least-squares technique to estimate the
received signal power model parameters, and the SNR
is estimated by eigendecomposition. To further enhance
the reliability, we use the prior knowledge of the signal
model parameter to constitute a constrained least-square
problem. We obtain the closed-form solution to this
problem by restating it as a projection problem onto a
convex slab.

B. Comparison With Some Benchmark Solvers
For comparable purposes, we first compare the proposed
path planning scheme with some benchmark design schemes,
including the logarithmic barrier function method and the
exhaustive search. The logarithmic barrier function method
is proposed in [20], which uses interior point methods to
solve a non-convex problem. Consequently, this solver only
guarantees a local optimal solution, and the performance of
this solver strongly hinges on the choice of the initial point
(in our simulations, pℓis initialized to pℓ−1 since pℓ−1 is
obviously an interior point). The second method is intuitively
simple. To obtain the optimal solution to the original problem,
we randomly generate one million feasible points and select
the best one that optimizes the objective function. Since we
searched extensively, the outcome produced by this algorithm
can be deemed a global optimum. The UCA’s array orien-
tation was selected at random. The line segment joining the
array and the emitter is perpendicular to the array baseline
of the ULA.
As can be observed in Fig. 3, the proposed path planning
method performs similarly to the exhaustive search. When we
repeat the randomization N = 50 times (marked by green
squares), the outcome generated from our proposed method
exhibits a close similarity to the optimal trajectory derived
from an exhaustive search. The logarithmic barrier function
method (marked by red hexagrams) can not approach the
optimal solution due to the entrapment in a local minimum.

VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
In this paper, we consider the path planning problem for a
single sensor array under several geometric constraints, and the
array orientation/attitude is considered. The problem is formu-
lated based on the A-optimality criterion and is addressed by
the SDR method. The problem is recast as a typical mixed
SD/SOCP problem and can be solved in polynomial time.
Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed path planning
scheme has near-optimal localization performance even in a
low number of randomizations. We will consider the robust
counterpart of the proposed problem to alleviate the impact of
sensor location errors in our future work.
