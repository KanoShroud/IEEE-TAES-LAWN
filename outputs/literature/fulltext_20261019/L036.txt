TDOA Source Localization in the Presence
of Synchronization Clock Bias and Sensor
Position Errors
Yue Wang, Student Member, IEEE, and K. C. Ho, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—TDOA positioning requires the sensors to be syn-
chronized when sampling the signals to locate an emitting source.
It also demands the sensor positions to be accurate. Achieving
synchronization may not be feasible when the sensors are widely
separated or are many, and precise sensor positions are often not
available in practice. This paper investigates TDOA localization
using a set of imperfect sensors, where timing synchronization
offsets and random position errors exist among different sensor
groups. The degradation in localization accuracy caused by syn-
chronization offsets and position errors is examined through the
Cramér–Rao lower bound (CRLB) analysis under Gaussian noise.
A novel method in obtaining sequentially algebraic solutions of
the source location, the sensor positions, and the synchronization
offsets is developed. Sequential estimation simpliﬁes the local-
ization task and offers complexity reduction. The performance
of the proposed estimates is shown to reach the CRLB accuracy
analytically using small noise analysis, although they are estimated
sequentially and not jointly. Simulations support and corroborate
the theoretical developments.
Index Terms—Algebraic solution, clock bias, localization, sensor
position uncertainty, TDOA.
I. INTRODUCTION
T
IME Difference of Arrival (TDOA) positioning is perhaps
one of the widely used techniques for the localization of
a signal source [1]–[4]. Unlike Time of Arrival (TOA) posi-
tioning, it does not require time stamp of the source signal and is
suitable for passive localization. It also achieves better localiza-
tion accuracy than other measurements such as Angle of Arrival
(AoA) and Received Signal Strength (RSS) in general [5], [6].
TDOA localization requires the sensors to be synchronized
in sampling the received signals. When the sensors are close to
each other, it is relatively simple to perform synchronous sam-
pling through the use of a single hardware with multichannel
acquisition capabilities. If the sensors are far away, achieving
synchronization will not be easy. Synchronizing the receivers
is known to be challenging in sensor networks due to a large
number of sensor nodes and their non-ideal clocks [7]–[12].
Without synchronous sampling, time offsets occur in TDOAs
and they can degrade the localization accuracy considerably.

Another challenge for TDOA localization is that the localiza-
tion performance is highly sensitive to the accuracy of sensor
positions. A small deviation of the sensor positions from the
actual could lead to a large amount of decrease in localization
performance [14], [20]. This is particularly obvious for locating
a distant source away from the sensors. Previous investigations
on the effect of sensor position errors assume the uncertainties
are completely random where no deterministic relationship ex-
ists among the errors from different sensors. In practice, nearby
sensors could be partially connected to each other physically,
such as in microphone array, and their position errors are re-
lated to each other. As a result, the degrees of freedom in sensor
position errors reduce and the performance damage from inac-
curate sensor positions could be less. This paper establishes a
fundamental investigation of the effect of timing synchroniza-
tion offsets and dependent sensor position errors on the TDOA
localization performance.
The localization scenario to be investigated consists of a
number of partially synchronized sensors that are used to locate
an emitting signal source through TDOA measurements. The
sensors are separated into many groups, they are synchronized
within each group and synchronization timing offsets occur
among different groups. The sensor positions are not known
precisely. Nevertheless, deterministic relationships exist among
the sensor position errors.
The study begins with the CRLB investigation to examine
the effect of synchronization timing offsets on the source local-
ization accuracy. The analysis indicates the performance degra-
dation is not affected by the amounts of the timing offsets, but
rather depends on the number of timing offsets. Furthermore,
the presence of clock offsets is equivalent to modifying the in-
verse of the TDOA covariance matrix through orthogonal pro-
jection.
The paper proceeds next to derive an estimator that is com-
putationally efﬁcient and in closed-form for determining the
source position, the reﬁned sensor positions and the synchro-
nization offsets. Instead of jointly estimating the three together
that could be complicated and computationally demanding, the
proposed estimator obtains ﬁrst the source location, the sensor
positions next and ﬁnally the timing offsets. Although the un-
knowns are estimated sequentially, we will show the interesting
result that all three unknowns attain the CRLB accuracy, as long
as the measurement noise and sensor position errors are not sig-
niﬁcant so that the nonlinear noise effect is negligible.
We would like to differentiate our studies here with the re-
lated works found in literature. The closed-form solutions in [1]

clock offsets. The algorithm is iterative and assumes the sensor
node positions are exactly known.
The contributions in the paper include:
• The CRLB study for the source location in the presence of
sensor clock and position errors;
• An algebraic closed-form estimator for the source location
estimate without requiring joint estimation with the clock
offsets and sensor positions;
• A solution of the sensor positions based on the source lo-
cation estimate;
• An estimator of the clock offsets using the obtained source
and sensor locations;
• Small noise analysis of the three proposed solutions in
reaching the CRLB accuracy under Gaussian noise.
The following section formulates the problem for investiga-
tion. Section III evaluates the CRLB of the estimation problem
and provides the interpretation of the effect of clock offsets.
Section IV proposes an algebraic closed-form estimator for the
source location. Section V validates analytically the source lo-
cation estimate is efﬁcient in achieving the CRLB performance.
Section VI develops the closed-form estimator for the sensor
positions that is based on the source position estimate and con-
ﬁrms its optimum accuracy in reaching the CRLB. Section VII
provides the algebraic solution of the timing offsets and cor-
roborates its optimum performance. Section VIII presents the
simulation results and Section IX is the conclusions.

IX. CONCLUSION
This paper investigates the problem of source localization in
the presence of sensor clock offsets and position errors, where
the clock offset is common for some sensors and deterministic

relationships are present for sensor positions. We ﬁnd that the
CRLB of the source position depends on the number of clock
offsets and does not depend on the actual clock offset values. In
addition, the effect of clock offsets is equivalent to increasing
the TDOA covariance matrix through an orthogonal projection
of its square root inverse. A sequential estimation method is de-
veloped that obtains the source position ﬁrst, the sensor posi-
tions next and ﬁnally the clock offset parameters. The proposed
estimator is algebraic and yields closed-form solution. Although
the estimation is sequential, the performance is shown in theory
and by simulations to reach the CRLB accuracy for all three sets
of parameters, before the nonlinear effect occurs when the noise
level becomes large.
