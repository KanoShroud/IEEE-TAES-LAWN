Abstract—The sensor position uncertainties and synchroniza-
tion offsets can cause substantial performance degradation if the
sensors are not properly calibrated. This paper investigates the
localization of a constant velocity moving target and the self-
calibration of sensors using a sequence of range and azimuth
measurements observed at successive instants. A theoretical study
by the Cramer-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) reveals that the sensor
positions can only be self-calibrated when there are at least
two sensors and synchronization offsets can be handled by joint
estimation. A low complexity sequential closed-form solution is
proposed to estimate the target position and velocity ﬁrst, and the
coordinates of each sensor and synchronization offset afterward.
While less intuitive, the analysis shows that the closed-form
solutions for both the target and sensor parameters can reach
the CRLB accuracy under small Gaussian noise. We also develop
a semideﬁnite programming (SDP) solution by semideﬁnite re-
laxation (SDR) for joint localization and calibration from the
Maximum Likelihood formulation, which exhibits higher noise
tolerance than the closed-form solution. Simulations validate the
analysis and the performance of the proposed methods.
Index Terms—Sensor calibration, range bias, sensor position
error, closed-form solution, semideﬁnite programming (SDP).
I. INTRODUCTION
T
ARGET localization and tracking using observations ac-
quired by several sensors is a fundamental problem for
many applications in radar, sonar, wireless sensor networks,
and Internet of Things (IoT) [1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6], [7]. By
exploiting the geometric relations with the sensors at known
positions, the target can be localized by range, angle, Doppler,
or other geometry-dependent measurements [8], [9], [10], [11].

Apart from the geometric factor, the target localization per-
formance is mostly dominated by the quality of measurements
and the precision of sensor positions. A number of methods
have been proposed to limit their inaccuracies on performance
loss by exploiting their statistical properties [12], [13], [14],
[15]. These methods include the Maximum Likelihood (ML)
estimator [14], [16], [17], [18], the closed-form solution [9],
[12], [19], [20], [21], [22], [23], and the semideﬁnite program-
ming (SDP) method [13], [24], [25], [26]. The ML estimator
maximizes the likelihood function constructed by the statistical
models of the measurements and sensor positions. It is often
expressed in an iterative form, requiring a good initial guess
and signiﬁcant amount of computation due to the nonlinear and
nonconvex relations between the measurements and unknowns.
The ML estimator is asymptotically efﬁcient. Applying it in
practice will require some levels of global convergence guar-
antee and divergence avoidance. The closed-form solutions,
such as the pseudolinear estimator [9], the instrumental variable
(IV) method [9], [19], and the two-step closed-form solution
[8], [12], [27], are based on the least squares (LS) technique
and offer computational efﬁciency. They can be regarded as
approximate realizations of the ML estimator [28], [29] and are
accurate under mild noise conditions. The SDP method [24],
[25], [30], [31] converts the nonconvex localization problem
to be convex by semideﬁnite relaxation (SDR) and obtains the
solution by a convex optimization software package. It can
achieve desirable performance even under large noise condi-
tions, at the cost of computational complexity. Unlike the ML
estimator, it ensures global convergence without falling into a
local minimum.
When the measurements are time-based, an additional chal-
lenge is the synchronization issue. Time difference of arrival
(TDOA) [8], [12], [30] measurements require precise synchro-
nization of the sensors, and time of arrival (TOA) [32], [33]
measurements demand additional synchronization with the tar-
get. Most research studies in the literature assume the sensors
are perfectly synchronized. Synchronization errors among the
sensors, however, often exist in practice due to clock drifts and
clock offsets [34], [35]. The time errors translate to biases in the
measurements that can lead to signiﬁcant performance drops.
As a result, joint synchronization and localization has been
considered to mitigate their detrimental effects [24], [30], [35],

[36], [37]. For example, the paper [36] proposed an iterative
ML algorithm based on alternating projection by estimating
one while ﬁxing the other. Lately, SDP solutions [24], [35]
were developed by relaxing the original nonconvex problem
for simultaneous joint estimation. These works [24], [35], [36]
assume the positions of the sensors are accurate. The studies
[30], [37], [38], [39] extended the research by accounting for
errors in the sensor positions. Speciﬁcally, [37] proposed a
computationally less demanding method than the ML estimator
by using the generalized total least squares (GTLS) technique,
but the performance is suboptimal and cannot reach the Cramer-
Rao Lower Bound (CRLB). In [38], by treating the sensor posi-
tion errors as noise terms and applying the weighted LS (WLS)
method, a new closed-form joint localization and synchroniza-
tion approach was proposed to estimate the user nodes position,
velocity and the clock parameters. The work [39] developed a
novel method of obtaining sequentially an algebraic solution
for the target position ﬁrst, and then the synchronization offset
with the target and the positions of the sensors.
The localization scenario in this paper consists of a number
of asynchronous sensors. The purpose is to locate a constant
velocity moving target by collecting a sequence of propaga-
tion time delays, equivalent to ranges with known propagation
speed, and azimuth angles at regular intervals. In addition to the
time offsets that lead to biases in the range measurements, the
precise sensor positions are not known. The task is to estimate
the position and velocity of the moving target, and the positions
and range biases of the sensors.
There are many works in the literature by using both range
related and arrival angle measurements for localization, includ-
ing [5], [6], [7], [11], [18], [22], [23], [26], [40]. They are either
considering the stationary object scenario or focusing on locat-
ing an object only rather than simultaneous object localization
and sensor self-calibration.
We begin with the CRLB investigation to examine the esti-
mation performance in such a scenario. The analysis illustrates
that while the target can be localized in the presence of sensor
position error(s) and unknown time offset(s) even with one
sensor, the sensor positions can be updated to be more accurate
only if there are two or more sensors. A low complexity closed-
form solution is then proposed to estimate the unknown param-
eters sequentially by using the WLS formulation. The method
ﬁrst estimates the target location parameters without involving
the estimation of range offsets and sensor positions. It then
determines the range bias and position of each sensor based
on the target position and velocity estimate from the ﬁrst step.
Although performing estimation sequentially, the novel analysis
shows that the closed-form solution with a special choice of the
weighting matrices can reach the CRLB accuracy for the target
and sensor parameters under small Gaussian noise. A beneﬁt to
the proposed sequential estimation method is that sensor self-
calibration can be made at a later time when the computational
resources are less demanding. In addition to the closed-form
solution, an SDP solution by the ML formulation for jointly
estimating all parameters together is proposed to extend the
optimum performance to a higher noise level than the closed-
form solution, at the expense of computational complexity.
The closest related work is [39] for the localization of a
stationary target using TDOAs only with synchronized sensor
groups. We address here a complicated scenario where the
target is moving, the sensors are not synchronized and the
measurements are ranges and azimuths over several instants.
The work [39] cannot be extended to the current scenario.
Although we use the sequential estimation approach, the closed-
form algorithm and analysis are different from [39] and more
involved, and [39] does not have the SDP solution. While [38]
addresses the moving target scenario, it uses TOA measure-
ments only and does not consider the calibration of sensor
positions.
We would like to highlight the contributions of this paper.
They include
• A proposition illustrating self-calibration of the sensor
positions is not possible with only one sensor, implying
that sensor position self-calibration is possible when there
are at least two sensors.
• A low complexity sequential closed-form solution for joint
object localization and sensor self-calibration, which sep-
arately estimates the target position and velocity ﬁrst and
then the sensor positions and range offsets.
• An analysis of the solution in reaching the CRLB perfor-
mance under mild Gaussian noise, although the estimation
was done sequentially rather than jointly.
• An SDP solution based on ML formulation for better noise
resilience, which is able to provide optimum performance
with a shorter observation period or a higher noise level
compared to the closed-form solution but at the expense
of higher complexity.
In the following, we ﬁrst give the measurement model and
problem formulation. Section III derives the CRLB for the
localization and self-calibration problem, and establishes an
interesting proposition related to sensor position estimation.
Section IV develops a closed-form solution that sequentially
estimates the position and velocity of the moving target, and
then the range bias and position of each sensor. Section V
derives the SDP solution for achieving higher noise tolerance.
Section VI presents the performance analysis of the closed-
form solution. Section VII shows the simulation results and
Section VIII concludes the paper.

VIII. CONCLUSION
The paper investigates the localization of a moving target
using a sequence of range and azimuth measurements in the
presence of synchronization offsets and inaccurate sensor posi-
tions. The CRLB analysis reveals that the sensor position can
be updated to be more accurate only when there are at least
two sensors. A sequential closed-form solution is proposed that
estimates the target position and velocity ﬁrst, and then the
position and range bias of each sensor. The analysis shows that
the closed-form solution in performing estimation sequentially
can reach the CRLB accuracy for all parameter estimates under
small Gaussian noise. An SDP solution for simultaneous joint
synchronization and localization is also formulated based on the
MLE and it can handle the problem better when the noise level
is high.
This work models the measurement bias explicitly for the
purpose of reaching an unbiased estimator, which will be useful
in target motion analysis or tracking where successive samples
can reduce the estimation variance but not the bias, resulting in
better estimate and avoiding the drift of a target track. In some
applications, the inherent bias in the measurements, such as
the case of RSS, may yield a bias estimate that has a mean-
square error lower than the CRLB. Whether it is better to model
the bias explicitly, one should consider the actual problem and
scenario, and the extent of bias in the measurements.
This work considered the 2-D scenario for ease of illustration.
The theoretical results of the three propositions are directly
applicable to 3-D, and the algorithms can be immediately ex-
tended to 3-D by including the pseudo-linear equations from
the elevation measurements [51] (eq. (5b)). The two proposed
solutions are batch estimators. It would be interesting and useful
to extend the methods for sequential real-time processing as a
new set of measurements from the next time step arrives.
